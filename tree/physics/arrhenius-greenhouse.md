In 1896 Svante Arrhenius calculated by hand how Earth's surface temperature would change if the carbon dioxide in the air were halved or doubled, using measurements of infrared absorption. He found about 5 to 6 degrees Celsius of warming for a doubling, a larger figure than the roughly 3 degrees of modern estimates. Guy Callendar (1938) linked measured warming to rising CO2.

Makoto Kobayashi and Toshihide Maskawa showed in 1973 that CP violation in the Standard Model needs at least three generations of quarks, when only three quarks were known. The bottom (1977) and top (1995) quarks were then found, and their mixing matrix, with a complex phase, describes how quarks change flavour in weak decays. They shared the 2008 Nobel Prize in Physics.

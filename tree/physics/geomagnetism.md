William Gilbert's De Magnete (1600) reported experiments on a spherical lodestone, which showed that a compass needle dips toward its poles just as it does toward the Earth's. He concluded that Earth itself is a giant magnet and separated magnetic attraction from the static electricity of rubbed amber. The book is an early model of experimental method.

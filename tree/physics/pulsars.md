Jocelyn Bell Burnell, a graduate student working with Antony Hewish, noticed in 1967 a radio source in the Cambridge survey that pulsed with a period of 1.337 seconds. The pulses come from a rapidly rotating neutron star whose radio beam sweeps past Earth. Pulsars were the first observational evidence for neutron stars, and Hewish shared the 1974 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Werner Heisenberg argued in 1927 that the position and momentum of a particle cannot both be sharply defined, and Earle Kennard soon proved the exact bound: the product of the two uncertainties is at least half the reduced Planck constant. The principle follows from the wave nature of matter and, with Bohr's complementarity, shaped the Copenhagen interpretation.

Pieter Zeeman observed in 1896 that spectral lines of sodium broaden when the light source is placed in a magnetic field. Hendrik Lorentz explained the effect with charged particles oscillating inside atoms, and the charge-to-mass ratio inferred for them was close to the value Thomson found for cathode rays in 1897. Zeeman and Lorentz shared the 1902 Nobel Prize in Physics.

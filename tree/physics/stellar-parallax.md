In 1838 Friedrich Bessel measured the yearly shift of 61 Cygni against more distant stars, about 0.3 arcseconds, which puts it near 10 light-years away. Thomas Henderson (Alpha Centauri) and Friedrich Struve (Vega) had results at about the same time. It was the first generally accepted distance to a star and ended the long search for parallax, the missing proof of Earth's motion.

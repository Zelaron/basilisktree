Lewis Fry Richardson worked out a forecast by hand during the First World War and published it in 1922, with an unrealistic result. In 1950 Jule Charney, Ragnar Fjørtoft and John von Neumann made the first successful computer forecast on ENIAC, using a simplified equation for the large-scale flow. Computer models of the atmosphere became the basis of modern weather prediction.

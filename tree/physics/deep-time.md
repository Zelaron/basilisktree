James Hutton's Theory of the Earth (1788) read the layers, folds and unconformities of rocks in Scotland as the product of slow, repeated cycles of erosion, deposition and uplift with no sign of a beginning. Charles Lyell's Principles of Geology (1830-33) made this uniformitarian view standard. It gave geology, and later evolutionary biology, the immense timescales they needed.

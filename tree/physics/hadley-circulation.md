Edmond Halley mapped the trade winds in 1686 and attributed them to the Sun heating the tropics. George Hadley (1735) argued that air moving toward the equator is deflected westward by Earth's rotation, which gives the easterly trades, in one circulation cell in each hemisphere. William Ferrel's three-cell picture (1856) later gave the tropical cell ending near 30 degrees.

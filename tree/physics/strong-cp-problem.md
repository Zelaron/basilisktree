Quantum chromodynamics allows a CP-violating parameter θ, yet the neutron's electric dipole moment, measured below 1.8 × 10⁻²⁶ e·cm (2020), forces θ below about 10⁻¹⁰. Why is it so small? The leading answer, the Peccei–Quinn mechanism (1977) with its axion, also supplies a dark-matter candidate; detecting the axion, or another mechanism that explains the tiny θ, would settle it.

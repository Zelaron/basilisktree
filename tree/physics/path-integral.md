Richard Feynman formulated quantum mechanics in 1948 as a sum over all possible paths between two points, each weighted by a phase given by the classical action divided by the reduced Planck constant. The idea built on Dirac's 1933 observation that the action governs quantum evolution. It leads directly to Feynman diagrams and is used throughout particle physics and statistical mechanics.

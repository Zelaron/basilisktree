After the Eightfold Way of Murray Gell-Mann and Yuval Ne'eman (1961) sorted the known hadrons into patterns, Gell-Mann and, independently, George Zweig proposed in 1964 that hadrons are made of three kinds of fractionally charged constituents, the quarks. The model accounted for the hadron spectrum and, after deep inelastic scattering in 1968, was accepted as real.

The Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation (1986) describes randomly growing interfaces such as burning paper fronts and bacterial colonies. In one dimension its exponents (growth exponent 1/3) and fluctuations are known exactly, but in two or more dimensions they are known only numerically, and whether there is an upper critical dimension is disputed. Exact exponents in 2+1 dimensions, or a proof of a closed formula, would settle it.

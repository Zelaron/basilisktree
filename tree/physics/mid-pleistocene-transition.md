Between about 1.2 and 0.8 million years ago, ice-age cycles switched from a 41,000-year rhythm to longer, stronger cycles averaging 100,000 years, with no matching change in orbital forcing; the 100,000-year cycle was identified in 1976. Was it declining CO2, erosion of regolith under ice sheets or internal dynamics? A model that reproduces the switch and its timing from physical drivers would settle it.

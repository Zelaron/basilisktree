In 2024 groups in Vienna and Braunschweig (Thorsten Schumm, Ekkehard Peik) and at JILA (Jun Ye) used lasers to excite the low-energy isomeric state of the thorium-229 nucleus, at about 8.4 electronvolts, the first laser excitation of an atomic nucleus. The transition could serve as a nuclear clock more stable than atomic clocks and test whether constants of nature vary.

Roger Penrose proved in 1965 that once a star has collapsed far enough to form a trapped surface, general relativity requires a singularity, a place where spacetime ends. With Stephen Hawking he extended the argument in 1970 to the expanding universe, which must then have begun in a singularity. The theorems showed that singularities are generic predictions of general relativity.

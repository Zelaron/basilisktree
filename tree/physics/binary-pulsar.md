Russell Hulse and Joseph Taylor found in 1974 a pulsar, PSR B1913+16, in a tight eight-hour orbit with another neutron star. The orbit shrinks by about 3.5 metres a year, within a fraction of a percent of the loss general relativity predicts from gravitational waves. It was the first, indirect, evidence for gravitational waves and earned the 1993 Nobel Prize in Physics.

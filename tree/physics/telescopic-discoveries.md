Galileo's Sidereus Nuncius (1610) reported mountains on the Moon, four moons orbiting Jupiter and many stars too faint to see with the naked eye; the phases of Venus followed later that year. These observations contradicted the Ptolemaic picture of perfect heavens circling Earth and gave strong evidence for the Copernican system.

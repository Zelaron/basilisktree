In 1247 Nasir al-Din al-Tusi described a device in which a circle rolling inside a circle twice its size makes a point on its rim move back and forth along a straight line. He and other astronomers of the Maragha school used it to build planetary models without Ptolemy's equant, and the same construction appears in Copernicus's De revolutionibus.

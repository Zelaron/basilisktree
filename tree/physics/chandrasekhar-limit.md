Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar showed in 1930-31, combining special relativity with Fermi-Dirac statistics, that a white dwarf cannot be heavier than about 1.4 solar masses, because degenerate electrons cannot support more. Heavier stellar cores must collapse further, and the limit sets the brightness of the Type Ia supernovae used to measure cosmic distances.

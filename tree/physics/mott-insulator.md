Nevill Mott pointed out in 1949 that a material with a half-filled electron band, which band theory says should be a metal, becomes an insulator when the electrons repel each other strongly enough to stay on their atoms. Nickel oxide is the classic example. Mott insulators are the parent materials of the high-temperature cuprate superconductors.

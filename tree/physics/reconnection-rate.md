Solar flares and magnetospheric substorms release magnetic energy far faster than the Sweet–Parker theory of reconnection (1957) allows. Simulations and spacecraft show a normalized rate of about 0.1 across many plasmas, and plasmoid instabilities and geometric arguments explain parts of it. A first-principles theory of the rate and of how reconnection switches on, tested against spacecraft data, would settle it.

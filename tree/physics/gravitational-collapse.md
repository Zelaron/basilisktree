In 1939 Richard Tolman, Robert Oppenheimer and George Volkoff calculated that a neutron star cannot exceed a maximum mass, then put at about 0.7 solar masses (modern estimates are 2 to 3). Oppenheimer and Hartland Snyder showed that a sufficiently massive star collapses without limit and seals itself off behind a horizon, the first picture of black hole formation.

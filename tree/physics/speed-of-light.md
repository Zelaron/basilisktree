Ole Rømer noticed in 1676 that eclipses of Jupiter's moon Io came later than predicted when Jupiter was farther from Earth, and concluded that light takes time to cross space. The delay implies a speed of roughly 220,000 km/s, about a quarter below the true value. Fizeau (1849) and Foucault (1862) later measured the speed on Earth.

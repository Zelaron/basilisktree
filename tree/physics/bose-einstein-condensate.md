Eric Cornell and Carl Wieman at JILA cooled rubidium atoms to about 170 nanokelvin in June 1995, and Wolfgang Ketterle at MIT did the same with sodium a few months later, producing a gas in which a large fraction of the atoms occupy one quantum state. It realized the state Bose and Einstein predicted 70 years earlier, and the three shared the 2001 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Henrietta Swan Leavitt, studying variable stars in the Magellanic Clouds at Harvard, reported in 1912 that brighter Cepheids pulse more slowly. Because the stars in each cloud are at nearly the same distance, the period tells the true brightness and so the distance, once calibrated. The relation became the first reliable standard candle for measuring distances to other galaxies.

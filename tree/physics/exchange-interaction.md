Werner Heisenberg showed in 1928 that ferromagnetism arises from the quantum mechanical exchange interaction, an electrostatic energy that depends on whether the spins of two electrons are parallel or antiparallel. This explained why iron and nickel can be permanently magnetized, which classical physics cannot, and gave the Heisenberg model of interacting spins.

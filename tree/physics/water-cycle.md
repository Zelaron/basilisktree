Pierre Perrault measured rainfall and the flow of the upper Seine in 1674 and found that the rain falling on the catchment was about six times what the river carried away, so rain alone can feed rivers. Edme Mariotte's measurements on the Seine at Paris agreed, and Edmond Halley measured evaporation from the sea (1687). Hydrology as a quantitative science began here.

Wolfgang Pauli proposed in 1930 that an undetected neutral particle carries off part of the energy in beta decay, and Enrico Fermi's theory of 1933-34 treated the decay as a neutron turning into a proton, an electron and a neutrino. It was the first theory of the weak interaction and it described the neutrino's role long before the particle was detected in 1956.

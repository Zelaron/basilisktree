Simulations of cold dark matter predict steeply rising 'cusps' of density at galaxy centres, but many dwarf galaxies show flat 'cores' (Flores and Primack; Moore, 1994). Is the difference caused by supernova feedback, self-interacting dark matter or something else? One model matching the full range of observed dwarf profiles, or data ruling a mechanism out, would settle it.

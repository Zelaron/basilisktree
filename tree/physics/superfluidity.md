Pyotr Kapitsa, and independently John Allen and Don Misener, found in 1937-38 that liquid helium cooled below 2.17 K flows through narrow channels with no measurable viscosity. Fritz London linked the effect to Bose-Einstein condensation (1938), and Lev Landau's two-fluid theory (1941) described its behaviour. It showed quantum mechanics at the scale of a visible liquid.

In the 1840s Julius Robert Mayer, James Joule and Hermann von Helmholtz established that heat, motion, electricity and chemical change are forms of one conserved quantity, energy. Joule measured the mechanical equivalent of heat in 1843-47, most famously with a paddle wheel, and Helmholtz stated the general law in 1847. It is the first law of thermodynamics and a pillar of physics.

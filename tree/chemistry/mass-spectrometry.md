Francis Aston's mass spectrograph (1919) sorted ions by mass and showed that most elements are mixtures of isotopes (Nobel Prize in Chemistry 1922), building on J. J. Thomson's ion-beam parabolas of 1912 to 1913. Mass spectrometry grew into one of the most sensitive and general analytical tools in chemistry, used from proteomics to drug testing, forensics and space missions.

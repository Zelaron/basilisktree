Henri Kagan found nonlinear effects in asymmetric catalysis in 1986, and in 1995 Kenso Soai discovered a reaction in which a tiny excess of one mirror-image product catalyses its own formation and grows to near purity. It is discussed as a model for how biological handedness arose, and brought Kagan and Soai the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

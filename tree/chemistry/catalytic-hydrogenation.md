Paul Sabatier and Jean-Baptiste Senderens showed in 1897 that finely divided nickel adds hydrogen to unsaturated organic compounds. Sabatier shared the 1912 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Hydrogenation hardens fats for margarine and makes fuels and fine chemicals, and the Sabatier reaction turns carbon dioxide and hydrogen into methane.

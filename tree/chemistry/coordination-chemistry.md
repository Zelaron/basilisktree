In 1893 Alfred Werner explained cobalt-ammonia salts as a central metal ion surrounded by six ligands at the corners of an octahedron. His coordination theory, which won the 1913 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, accounts for the structure, colour and reactivity of transition-metal compounds.

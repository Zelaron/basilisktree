Leo Baekeland combined phenol and formaldehyde under heat and pressure to make a hard resin that no longer softens when reheated (1907, patented 1909), the first fully synthetic plastic. Bakelite insulated electrical parts and moulded radios, telephones and appliances, and opened the era of plastics made entirely from chemicals.

E. J. Corey formalised in 1967 the planning of an organic synthesis by working backwards from the target molecule, cutting it at strategic bonds into simpler pieces. His LHASA program (1969) was an early computer synthesis planner, and retrosynthetic analysis is how chemists teach and design syntheses; Corey won the 1990 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

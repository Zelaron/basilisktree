Clemens Roothaan and George Hall independently showed in 1951 that expanding molecular orbitals in a finite set of basis functions turns the Hartree-Fock equations into matrix equations that a computer can solve. Frank Boys's use of Gaussian functions (1950) made the integrals fast to compute, and the approach underlies nearly every ab initio program.

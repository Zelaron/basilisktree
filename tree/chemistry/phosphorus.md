Hennig Brand, a Hamburg merchant searching for the philosopher's stone, boiled down large amounts of urine and obtained a white substance that glowed in the dark (1669). It was the first element whose discoverer is known, and phosphorus later became central to matches, fertilizers and biochemistry.

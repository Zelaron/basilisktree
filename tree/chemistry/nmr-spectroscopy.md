After Felix Bloch and Edward Purcell detected nuclear magnetic resonance in 1946, chemists found that a nucleus's resonance frequency depends on its chemical surroundings (1950). The three-peak ethanol spectrum of Arnold, Dharmatti and Packard (1951) made the point, and NMR became the main method for working out molecular structures. Its imaging offspring is MRI.

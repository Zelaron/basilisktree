Justus von Liebig's five-bulb Kaliapparat trapped the carbon dioxide from a burned sample in potash solution (1831), so that carbon and hydrogen in an organic compound could be weighed accurately within hours. Routine elemental analysis supplied the data on which structural organic chemistry was built.

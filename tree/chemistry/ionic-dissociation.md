Svante Arrhenius proposed in his 1884 thesis and a paper of 1887 that salts, acids and bases split into charged ions in water even without a current. The theory explained electrical conductivity, osmotic pressure and the lowering of freezing points, and won him the 1903 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Potters in Zhejiang fired porcelain stone and clay above about 1,260 degrees Celsius to make true porcelain by the late Eastern Han (about 150 CE), a hard, white, translucent ceramic. Chinese porcelain became a major export for a thousand years, and Böttger and Tschirnhaus in Saxony reinvented it in Europe in 1708.

Neil Bartlett found in 1962 that platinum hexafluoride oxidises xenon to an orange-yellow solid that he formulated as xenon hexafluoroplatinate, overturning the belief that the noble gases are chemically inert. Xenon fluorides were made within months, and the discovery reshaped textbooks and ideas about bonding.

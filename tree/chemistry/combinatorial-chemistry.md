Árpád Furka devised split-and-pool synthesis on beads in the 1980s and published it in 1991, alongside Kit Lam's and Richard Houghten's peptide library methods; a few steps make millions of compounds. Combinatorial libraries fed high-throughput screening for drugs, and the idea returned in DNA-encoded libraries.

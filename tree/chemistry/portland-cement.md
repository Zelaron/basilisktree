Joseph Aspdin patented a cement made by burning limestone and clay in 1824 and named it after Portland stone. Isaac Johnson's hotter firing to a hard clinker in the 1840s gave the modern product. Mixed with sand, gravel and water it makes concrete, and about four billion tonnes of cement are now made each year.

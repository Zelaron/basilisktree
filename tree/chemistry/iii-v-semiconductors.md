Heinrich Welker at Siemens showed in 1952 that compounds of group III and group V elements, such as gallium arsenide and indium antimonide, are semiconductors with useful electronic properties. Direct-gap III-V materials went on to make LEDs, laser diodes, space solar cells and fast radio-frequency chips.

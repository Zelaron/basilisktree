John Pople's group released Gaussian 70 in 1970, a program that packaged ab initio quantum chemistry with standard basis sets so that any chemist could compute molecular energies and structures. Systematic model chemistries and later versions made computation a routine partner to experiment; Pople shared the 1998 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

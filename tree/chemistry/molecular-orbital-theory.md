Friedrich Hund and Robert Mulliken described electrons in molecules as occupying orbitals spread over the whole molecule (1927 to 1928), and John Lennard-Jones's linear combination of atomic orbitals (1929) made the idea computable. Mulliken named the orbital in 1932 and won the 1966 Nobel Prize in Chemistry; molecular orbital theory underlies most computational chemistry.

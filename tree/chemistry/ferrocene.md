Peter Pauson and Thomas Kealy made an unexpectedly stable orange iron compound in 1951. Geoffrey Wilkinson and Ernst Otto Fischer independently worked out in 1952 that it is an iron atom sandwiched between two cyclopentadienyl rings. Ferrocene launched modern organometallic chemistry, and Wilkinson and Fischer shared the 1973 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

How do the heaviest elements, 112 to 118, bond when relativistic effects reshape their electron shells? Pitzer asked in 1975 whether elements 112, 114 and 118 would be nearly inert gases, and single-atom experiments since 2007 find copernicium and flerovium volatile but metallic. A solution is relativistic quantum chemistry whose predictions for all of 112–118, including elements not yet studied chemically, match such experiments.

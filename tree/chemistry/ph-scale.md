Søren Sørensen, working at the Carlsberg Laboratory on brewing and enzymes, defined pH as the negative logarithm of the hydrogen-ion concentration in 1909. The scale made acidity a number that could be measured and controlled in laboratories, industry, medicine and agriculture; glass electrodes later turned it into a meter reading.

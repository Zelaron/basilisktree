At IBM in 1982, Hiroshi Ito and Grant Willson, with Jean Fréchet, devised photoresists in which one acid molecule released by light triggers hundreds of chemical reactions in a polymer film. These chemically amplified resists made deep-ultraviolet lithography practical and became the standard for patterning chips.

Rudolph Marcus showed in 1956 that the rate of electron transfer between molecules depends on how much the solvent must rearrange, and predicted an inverted region in which a larger driving force slows the reaction. Experiments confirmed this in 1984. The theory underlies electrochemistry and photosynthesis research and won the 1992 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

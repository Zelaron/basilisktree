Robert Boyle's The Sceptical Chymist (1661) attacked the four Aristotelian elements and Paracelsus's three principles and argued that chemical claims should rest on experiment and on a corpuscular picture of matter. He also proposed an early modern definition of an element, and the book helped turn alchemy into chemistry.

Otto Diels and Kurt Alder reported in 1928 that a diene and an alkene join in one step to form a six-membered ring, with predictable stereochemistry. The Diels-Alder reaction became one of the most reliable ring-forming reactions in synthesis, used for steroids, vitamins and polymers, and won them the 1950 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Peter Debye and Paul Scherrer in Göttingen (1916) and Albert Hull in the United States (1917) recorded X-ray diffraction rings from powdered samples, so that no single crystal was needed. Powder patterns act as fingerprints of crystalline phases and are the routine tool for identifying minerals, drugs, catalysts and battery materials.

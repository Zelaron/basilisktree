Max Bodenstein explained the reaction of hydrogen and chlorine under light in 1913 as a chain of steps in which each reactive intermediate produces another. Nikolay Semenov and Cyril Hinshelwood extended the idea to branching chains that explain flames and explosions, and shared the 1956 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

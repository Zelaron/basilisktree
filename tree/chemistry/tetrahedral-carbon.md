Jacobus van 't Hoff and Joseph Le Bel proposed independently in 1874 that the four bonds of a carbon atom point to the corners of a tetrahedron. A carbon bearing four different groups then comes in two mirror-image forms, which explained Pasteur's optical isomers and founded stereochemistry. Van 't Hoff won the first Nobel Prize in Chemistry (1901).

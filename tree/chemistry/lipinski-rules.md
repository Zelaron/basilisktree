Christopher Lipinski observed in 1997 that most orally active drugs have a molecular weight below 500, a logP below 5 and no more than five hydrogen-bond donors and ten acceptors. His rule of five became a standard filter for drug-likeness in screening libraries and in the design of drug candidates.

Irving Langmuir modelled adsorption in 1916 to 1918 as molecules sticking to a fixed number of identical sites on a surface, forming at most one layer. His isotherm and picture of surface reactions founded surface chemistry and the kinetics of heterogeneous catalysis, and he received the 1932 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

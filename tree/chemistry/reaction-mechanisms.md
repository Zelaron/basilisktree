Christopher Ingold and Edward Hughes showed from rate laws and stereochemistry in 1935 that nucleophilic substitution proceeds by two routes: SN2, in one concerted step with inversion of configuration, and SN1, through a carbocation intermediate. Their work founded physical organic chemistry, the study of how and why reactions happen.

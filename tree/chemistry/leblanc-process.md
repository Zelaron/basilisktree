Nicolas Leblanc patented a way to make soda ash (sodium carbonate) from salt, sulfuric acid, chalk and coal in 1791. It supplied glass, soap, paper and textile makers for much of the 19th century, and its hydrogen chloride fumes led to Britain's Alkali Act of 1863. Solvay's ammonia-soda process (1861) eventually replaced it.

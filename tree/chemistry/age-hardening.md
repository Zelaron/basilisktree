Alfred Wilm found in 1906 that an aluminium alloy with copper and magnesium, quenched from a high temperature, keeps hardening for days at room temperature. Sold as Duralumin from 1909, it was the first high-strength aluminium alloy and went into Zeppelins and then all-metal aircraft; fine precipitates inside the metal cause the hardening.

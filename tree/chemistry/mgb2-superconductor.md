Jun Akimitsu's group in Japan found in 2001 that magnesium diboride, a cheap compound known since the 1950s, becomes superconducting at 39 K, the highest transition temperature of any conventional phonon-driven superconductor at ambient pressure. MgB2 wires are used in some magnets, and the discovery challenged ideas about the limits of conventional superconductivity.

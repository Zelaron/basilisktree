K. Barry Sharpless called in 2001 for modular, high-yield reactions that join building blocks simply and safely, and the copper-catalysed azide-alkyne cycloaddition (Morten Meldal, and Valery Fokin with Sharpless, 2002) became the archetype. Carolyn Bertozzi's bioorthogonal versions work in living cells; Sharpless, Meldal and Bertozzi won the 2022 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

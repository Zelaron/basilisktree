Georg Wittig showed in 1954 that a phosphorus ylide converts a ketone or aldehyde into an alkene, placing the new carbon-carbon double bond exactly where the carbonyl group was. The Wittig reaction is a standard tool for making alkenes, used industrially for vitamin A, and brought Wittig a share of the 1979 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

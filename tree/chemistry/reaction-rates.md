Ludwig Wilhelmy followed the acid-catalysed breakdown of sucrose with a polarimeter and wrote its rate as a differential equation (1850), the first quantitative study of how fast a chemical reaction goes. His work was overlooked for decades but is now counted as the start of chemical kinetics.

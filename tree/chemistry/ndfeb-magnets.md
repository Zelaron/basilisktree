Masato Sagawa at Sumitomo and John Croat at General Motors independently found in 1983 to 1984 that an alloy of neodymium, iron and boron, Nd2Fe14B, makes a permanent magnet far stronger than earlier ones. Neodymium magnets are used in hard drives, electric-vehicle motors, wind turbines and headphones.

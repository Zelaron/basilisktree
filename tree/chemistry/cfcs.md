Thomas Midgley and his team at General Motors made chlorofluorocarbons in 1928 as non-toxic, non-flammable refrigerants and sold them as Freon from 1930. They replaced ammonia and sulfur dioxide in refrigerators and air conditioners and served as aerosol propellants; their stability later let them reach the stratosphere and destroy ozone.

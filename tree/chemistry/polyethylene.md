ICI chemists Eric Fawcett and Reginald Gibson obtained a waxy white solid from ethylene gas at about 1,400 atmospheres in March 1933, in an experiment on high-pressure reactions. The polymer, polyethylene, insulated radar cables in the Second World War and is now the most-produced plastic, used in bags, bottles, pipes and films.

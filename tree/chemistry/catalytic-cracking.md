Eugene Houdry's process passed heated heavy oil over a clay catalyst, splitting large molecules into high-octane gasoline in commercial units from 1936 to 1937. It supplied aviation fuel to the Allied air forces in the Second World War; fluid catalytic cracking, and zeolite catalysts from the 1960s, later took over and remain central to gasoline production.

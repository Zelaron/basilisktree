The absorbance of light by a solution grows in proportion to the concentration of the absorbing substance and the length of the light path. August Beer stated the concentration law in 1852, extending work by Bouguer (1729) and Lambert (1760); it underlies colorimeters, UV-visible spectrometers and much of clinical chemistry.

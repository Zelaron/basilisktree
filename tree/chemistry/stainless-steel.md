Harry Brearley, looking for a better gun-barrel steel in Sheffield, found in 1913 that steel with about 12 percent chromium resisted corrosion and staining. Chromium forms a thin, self-repairing oxide film, and stainless steels went on to cutlery, kitchens, chemical plants, medical instruments and buildings. Krupp's chromium-nickel grades appeared at about the same time.

Gilbert N. Lewis proposed in 1916 that a covalent bond is a pair of electrons shared between two atoms, and drew atoms with dots for their outer electrons. Irving Langmuir's octet rule (1919) extended the idea, and Lewis dot structures remain the first way chemists draw and reason about bonding.

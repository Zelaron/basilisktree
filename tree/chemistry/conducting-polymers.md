Alan Heeger, Alan MacDiarmid and Hideki Shirakawa found in 1977 that polyacetylene doped with iodine conducts electricity almost like a metal. Conducting polymers opened organic electronics, with uses in antistatic coatings, organic light-emitting diodes and flexible devices, and the three shared the 2000 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

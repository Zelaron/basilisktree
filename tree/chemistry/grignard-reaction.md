Victor Grignard showed in 1900 that magnesium metal inserts into carbon-halogen bonds to give organomagnesium reagents, which add carbon chains to carbonyl compounds. It gave chemists a general and convenient way to form carbon-carbon bonds, won Grignard the 1912 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, and is still used in laboratories and industry.

Bruce Merrifield attached the first amino acid of a peptide to insoluble polymer beads in 1963 and added further residues one at a time, washing away excess reagents after each step. Solid-phase synthesis made peptides, and later DNA and RNA, quick to make and automatable, and won him the 1984 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

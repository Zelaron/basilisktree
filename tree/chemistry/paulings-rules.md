Linus Pauling proposed five rules in 1929 for how ions pack into ionic crystals: the coordination polyhedron around each ion follows from the ratio of ion radii, the charges around each ion should balance, and sharing of polyhedron edges and faces is unfavourable. They guided the interpretation of oxide and silicate structures for decades.

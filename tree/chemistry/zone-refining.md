William Pfann showed in 1952 that sweeping a narrow molten zone along a solid bar carries impurities with it to one end, purifying the material with each pass. Zone refining gave the germanium and silicon, pure to parts per billion, that early transistors needed, and it is still used for ultrapure metals and semiconductors.

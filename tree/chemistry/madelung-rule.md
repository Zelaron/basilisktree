Why do electrons fill atomic subshells in order of increasing n + ℓ, and for equal n + ℓ in order of increasing n, with exceptions such as chromium and copper? Löwdin challenged theorists in 1969 to derive this Madelung rule from quantum mechanics, and the proposed derivations remain contested. A solution derives the rule from the many-electron Schrödinger equation, or a controlled approximation to it, and predicts where it fails.

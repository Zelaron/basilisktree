G. H. Hardy and Wilhelm Weinberg showed independently in 1908 that in a large randomly mating population, allele and genotype frequencies stay constant from generation to generation unless something acts on them. It answered the objection that dominant alleles must spread, and serves as the baseline model of population genetics.

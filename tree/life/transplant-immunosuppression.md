Jean-François Borel at Sandoz found in 1972 that ciclosporin, made by a fungus from Norwegian soil, suppresses the immune system, and Roy Calne at Cambridge used it in transplant patients from 1978. It acts selectively on T cells, raised one-year survival of kidney grafts from about 50% to above 80%, and made heart, liver and lung transplants practical.

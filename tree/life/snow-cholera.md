During the 1854 cholera outbreak in Soho, London, John Snow plotted deaths on a map, traced them to the Broad Street water pump and persuaded the parish to remove its handle. His argument that cholera spreads through contaminated water, made decades before germs were accepted, is a founding case of modern epidemiology.

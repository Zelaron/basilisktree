In 1914 Albert Hustin in Brussels and Luis Agote in Buenos Aires used sodium citrate to stop donated blood clotting, and Richard Lewisohn in New York soon refined it. Blood could then be stored and carried to the patient, which made transfusion routine in surgery and, in the First World War, on the battlefield.

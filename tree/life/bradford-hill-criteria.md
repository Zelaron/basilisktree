In a 1965 address Austin Bradford Hill listed nine considerations for judging whether an association between a factor and a disease is causal: strength, consistency, specificity, temporality, biological gradient, plausibility, coherence, experiment and analogy. They remain a standard guide for epidemiologists weighing evidence.

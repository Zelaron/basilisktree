Gerhard Domagk at Bayer showed in 1932 that the red dye Prontosil cured streptococcal infection in mice, and published in 1935 after successful clinical use. A Pasteur Institute team found that the active part is sulfanilamide. Sulfa drugs were the first broadly effective antibacterial medicines, and Domagk won the 1939 Nobel Prize.

Emile Zuckerkandl and Linus Pauling compared haemoglobins of different species and found in 1962 that amino acid differences grow roughly in step with time since divergence. Molecules could therefore date evolutionary branch points, and the molecular clock became a main tool for reconstructing the history of life.

Leland Hartwell found cell-division-cycle genes in yeast in the 1970s, Paul Nurse identified the key kinase cdc2 in fission yeast, and Tim Hunt discovered in 1983 cyclin proteins that rise and are destroyed in each cycle of sea urchin eggs. Cyclin-dependent kinases drive the cell through division, and their misregulation is central to cancer. The three shared the 2001 Nobel Prize.

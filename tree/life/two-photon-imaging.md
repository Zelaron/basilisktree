Winfried Denk, James Strickler and Watt Webb showed in 1990 that a pulsed infrared laser can excite fluorescent dyes only at the focus of a microscope, through the near-simultaneous absorption of two photons. The method images deep into living tissue with little damage, and it later allowed calcium signals of hundreds of neurons to be watched in the brains of living animals.

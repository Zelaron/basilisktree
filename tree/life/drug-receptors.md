John Newport Langley proposed in 1905, from the actions of nicotine and curare on muscle, that cells carry receptive substances that drugs combine with. Paul Ehrlich's earlier side-chain theory had suggested binding sites for toxins and dyes. The receptor idea became the basis of pharmacology and of drug design.

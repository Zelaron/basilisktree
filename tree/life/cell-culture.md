George Gey's laboratory at Johns Hopkins grew cells from the cervical tumour of Henrietta Lacks, taken without her knowledge in 1951, and they proved to divide without limit. HeLa cells became the standard human cell line, used for the polio vaccine, cancer research and countless other studies, and their history shaped debate on consent.

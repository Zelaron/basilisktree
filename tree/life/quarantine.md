In 1377 the Republic of Ragusa (now Dubrovnik) ordered travellers from plague-stricken places to wait thirty days outside the city before entering. The period was later lengthened to forty days, which gave quarantine its name. It was one of the first public-health laws, and isolation of travellers and the sick has been a basic response to epidemics ever since.

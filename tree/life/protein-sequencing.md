Frederick Sanger worked out the complete amino acid sequence of insulin between 1951 and 1955, using a chemical label for the end of each chain and partial digestion into fragments. It showed that a protein has a precise, unique sequence, and it paved the way for the study of how genes specify proteins. He received the 1958 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

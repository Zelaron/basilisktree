In 1950 Richard Doll and Austin Bradford Hill, and independently Ernest Wynder and Evarts Graham, found in case-control studies that lung cancer patients were far more often heavy smokers. Doll and Hill's British doctors cohort, begun in 1951, confirmed the link. These studies changed what epidemiology could establish and led to tobacco control.

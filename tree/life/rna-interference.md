Andrew Fire and Craig Mello showed in 1998 that double-stranded RNA injected into the nematode worm silences the gene with the matching sequence far more efficiently than single strands. RNA interference became a standard way to switch off any gene, and led to a class of drugs, the first of which was approved in 2018. They won the 2006 Nobel Prize.

Otto Loewi showed in 1921 that fluid from a stimulated frog heart slowed a second heart, so nerves signal by releasing a chemical, which Henry Dale identified as acetylcholine. The two shared the 1936 Nobel Prize. It settled the long debate over whether most synapses work by chemicals or by electricity.

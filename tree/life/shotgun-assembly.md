In 1995 a team at The Institute for Genomic Research, led by Craig Venter, Claire Fraser and Hamilton Smith with Robert Fleischmann as first author, broke the 1.8 million bases of Haemophilus influenzae into random fragments, sequenced them and reassembled the genome by computer. It was the first complete genome of a free-living organism and proved the whole-genome shotgun strategy.

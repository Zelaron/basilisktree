Robin Holliday and John Pugh, and independently Arthur Riggs, proposed in 1975 that methylation of cytosine in DNA can be copied when DNA replicates, so that a pattern of gene silencing is inherited through cell division. The idea of heritable chemical marks beyond the DNA sequence founded the modern study of epigenetics.

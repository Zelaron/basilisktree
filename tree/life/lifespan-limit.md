James Fries argued in 1980 that human life span is fixed, with an ideal average near 85 years. Death rates appear to stop rising after about 108, which would mean no detectable ceiling. Solving it means settling, on validated extreme-age records, whether the force of mortality plateaus and whether a finite bound exists. Jeanne Calment's 122 years, reached in 1997, is still the record.

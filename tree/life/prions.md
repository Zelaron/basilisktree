Stanley Prusiner proposed in 1982 that scrapie, a fatal brain disease of sheep, is caused by a misfolded protein that converts normal copies of itself and contains no nucleic acid. The idea met fierce scepticism and was later accepted, since the same mechanism explains Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease and mad cow disease. Prusiner won the 1997 Nobel Prize.

François Jacob and Jacques Monod proposed in 1961 that genes are switched on and off by repressor proteins that bind DNA near them, using the lac operon of E. coli, which makes lactose-digesting enzymes only when lactose is present. It introduced gene regulation and the idea of messenger RNA, and they shared the 1965 Nobel Prize.

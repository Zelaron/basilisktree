Barbara McClintock found in maize in the late 1940s that some genetic elements move from place to place on chromosomes and switch neighbouring genes on and off, and described them in 1950. Her results met scepticism for decades. Transposable elements are now known to make up nearly half of the human genome, and she won the 1983 Nobel Prize.

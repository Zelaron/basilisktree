Neolithic surgeons cut or scraped openings in the skulls of living people, and healed bone around the edges shows that many patients survived. A skull from Ensisheim in Alsace, dated to about 5100 BCE, is a well-documented early case. Motives probably included head injuries, headaches and ritual.

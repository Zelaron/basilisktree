Louis Pasteur and Émile Roux weakened rabies by drying the spinal cords of infected rabbits, and in July 1885 used the product to treat Joseph Meister, a nine-year-old bitten by a rabid dog, who survived. It was the first vaccine made in the laboratory against a viral disease of humans, and it showed that vaccines could be produced by design.

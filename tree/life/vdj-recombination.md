Susumu Tonegawa showed in 1976 that the DNA encoding antibodies is rearranged as B cells develop: gene segments are cut out and joined, so the antibody genes of a mature lymphocyte differ from those in other cells. Combining a few hundred segments produces an enormous number of different antibodies. He won the 1987 Nobel Prize.

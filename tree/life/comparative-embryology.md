Karl Ernst von Baer discovered the mammalian egg in 1827 and in 1828 set out his laws of development: embryos of different animals share general features early and diverge into specialised forms. His laws undercut the idea that embryos pass through the adult forms of lower animals, and became the basis of comparative embryology.

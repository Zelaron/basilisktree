Medieval farmers left a third or half of their land fallow each year. The Norfolk four-course system, popularised by Charles Townshend from about 1730, replaced fallow with a cycle of wheat, turnips, barley and clover that fed livestock and restored soil nitrogen. Yields and herds grew, a foundation of the British agricultural revolution.

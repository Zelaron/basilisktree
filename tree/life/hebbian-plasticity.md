In The Organization of Behavior (1949) Donald Hebb proposed that when one cell repeatedly helps to fire another, the connection between them strengthens. The postulate, often summarised as cells that fire together wire together, guided later work on learning and memory and is an ancestor of learning rules in artificial neural networks.

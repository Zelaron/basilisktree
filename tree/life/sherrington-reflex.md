Charles Sherrington's The Integrative Action of the Nervous System (1906) described how reflexes combine excitation and inhibition, and how nerve cells communicate across junctions that he had named synapses. His work established the reflex arc as the unit of nervous integration and earned him the 1932 Nobel Prize.

Eduard Buchner showed in 1897 that juice pressed from ground yeast, with no living cells in it, still fermented sugar. Fermentation was therefore chemistry done by molecules, which came to be called enzymes, and the finding opened biochemistry. Buchner won the 1907 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

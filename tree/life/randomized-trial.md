The Medical Research Council trial of streptomycin for tuberculosis, designed by Austin Bradford Hill and reported in 1948, allocated patients to treatment or bed rest by sealed envelopes drawn from a random sequence, so that neither doctors nor patients chose. It became the model for the randomised controlled trial, now the standard test of whether a treatment works.

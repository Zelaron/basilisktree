At the Vienna General Hospital, Ignaz Semmelweis noticed that women died of childbed fever far more often in the clinic staffed by doctors fresh from autopsies. After he ordered chlorinated-lime handwashing in May 1847, deaths there fell from 18% in April to about 2% by June. Colleagues rejected his explanation, and it was accepted only after germ theory.

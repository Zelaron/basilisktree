J. Andrew McCammon, Bruce Gelin and Martin Karplus simulated the motions of a small protein, bovine pancreatic trypsin inhibitor, for 9.2 picoseconds on a computer in 1977. It showed that proteins constantly flex and fluctuate, and began the computational study of biomolecules honoured by the 2013 Nobel Prize in Chemistry (Karplus, Michael Levitt and Arieh Warshel).

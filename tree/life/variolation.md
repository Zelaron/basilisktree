Inoculating a healthy person with matter from a mild smallpox case was practised in Asia, Africa and the Ottoman Empire. Lady Mary Wortley Montagu brought it to Britain, and in 1721 Zabdiel Boylston and Cotton Mather, who had learned of it from the enslaved man Onesimus, applied it in Boston. About 2% of those inoculated died, against up to 30% in natural smallpox.

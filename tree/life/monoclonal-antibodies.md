Georges Köhler and César Milstein fused antibody-making B cells with myeloma tumour cells in 1975 to create hybridomas, immortal lines that each make one chosen antibody in unlimited amounts. Monoclonal antibodies became standard reagents and diagnostics and the basis of many of today's best-selling drugs. They shared the 1984 Nobel Prize.

Félix d'Hérelle reported in 1917 that an invisible agent in filtrates could dissolve bacterial cultures, and named it bacteriophage ('bacteria eater'). Frederick Twort had seen the effect in 1915. Phages were tried as therapy, then neglected after antibiotics, and became the main experimental system of early molecular biology.

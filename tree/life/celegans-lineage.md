John Sulston and colleagues traced the divisions of every cell in the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans from egg to adult (1983), finding that the lineage of all 959 body cells of the hermaphrodite is the same in every animal. Some cells were found to be programmed to die, and Sulston, Robert Horvitz and Sydney Brenner shared the 2002 Nobel Prize.

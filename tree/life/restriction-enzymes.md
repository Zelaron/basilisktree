Werner Arber described bacterial enzymes that cut invading DNA, and in 1970 Hamilton Smith isolated one, HindII, that cuts DNA at a fixed sequence. Daniel Nathans used such enzymes to map the SV40 virus genome. Restriction enzymes became the scissors of recombinant DNA, and the three shared the 1978 Nobel Prize.

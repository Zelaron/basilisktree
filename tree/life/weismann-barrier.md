In Das Keimplasma (1892) August Weismann argued that heredity passes only through the germ line, and that changes acquired by the body, such as the effects of use or injury, do not travel back to the eggs and sperm. His experiment of cutting the tails off mice for many generations supported the case against Lamarckian inheritance.

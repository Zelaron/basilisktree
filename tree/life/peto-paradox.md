A whale has vastly more cells than a mouse and lives far longer, yet big, long-lived species do not get proportionally more cancer, as Richard Peto noted in 1977. Zoo studies from 2022 to 2025 disagree on whether risk rises with body mass at all, and a 2025 Bayesian reanalysis found the samples too small to decide. Solving it means settling the scaling and identifying the defences, such as extra TP53 copies in elephants, that explain it.

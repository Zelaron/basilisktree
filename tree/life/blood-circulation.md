William Harvey calculated that the heart ejects far more blood each hour than the body could possibly make, and showed with ligature experiments that blood flows from the arteries through the tissues to the veins and back to the heart. His De Motu Cordis (1628) replaced Galen's account and is an early example of quantitative reasoning in physiology.

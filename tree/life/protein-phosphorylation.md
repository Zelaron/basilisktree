Edmond Fischer and Edwin Krebs showed in 1955 that the enzyme glycogen phosphorylase is switched from an inactive to an active form by the addition of a phosphate group, and later that this is controlled by a kinase responding to hormones. Reversible phosphorylation became the main way cells regulate proteins and relay signals, and they won the 1992 Nobel Prize.

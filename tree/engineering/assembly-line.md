Henry Ford's engineers, among them Charles Sorensen, introduced a moving assembly line for the Model T at Highland Park, Michigan, in 1913. Chassis assembly time fell from about twelve hours to about ninety minutes, cutting the car's price until ordinary families could buy it. Mass production then spread to every large industry.

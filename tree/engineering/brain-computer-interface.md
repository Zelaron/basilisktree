In 2006 Matthew Nagle, paralysed by a spinal injury, used a 96-electrode array implanted in his motor cortex by the BrainGate team led by John Donoghue and Leigh Hochberg to move a computer cursor and operate a robotic hand with his thoughts. Later participants controlled robot arms and typed, and brain implants are now being tested to restore movement and speech.

Charles Kao and George Hockham argued in 1966 that glass fibre could carry light over long distances if its impurities were removed, and in 1970 Robert Maurer, Donald Keck and Peter Schultz at Corning made fibre with a loss below 20 decibels per kilometre. Fibre now carries nearly all long-distance data, including undersea cables, and Kao shared the 2009 Nobel Prize in Physics.

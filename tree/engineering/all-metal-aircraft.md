Hugo Junkers's J 1, which first flew in December 1915, was the first all-metal aircraft, a cantilever monoplane of sheet iron with no wing bracing wires. Metal skins carried loads better than fabric over wood, and stressed-skin airliners such as the Douglas DC-3 of 1935 followed, making aircraft stronger, more durable and more economical.

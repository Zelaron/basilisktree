Edwin Howard Armstrong devised the superheterodyne receiver in 1918 while serving in France. It mixes the incoming radio signal with a local oscillator to shift it to one fixed intermediate frequency, where it can be filtered and amplified with high gain and selectivity, and nearly every radio, television and mobile phone receiver still works this way.

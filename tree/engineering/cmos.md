Frank Wanlass at Fairchild, working with Chih-Tang Sah, combined n-type and p-type MOS transistors into a complementary pair in 1963, a circuit that draws almost no current except while switching. This low power use let millions and later billions of transistors share a chip without overheating, and CMOS became the basis of nearly all digital integrated circuits.

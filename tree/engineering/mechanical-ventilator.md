Philip Drinker and Louis Agassiz Shaw built the iron lung in 1928, a chamber that lowered the pressure around the chest to draw air in, and used it for polio patients who could not breathe. In the Copenhagen polio epidemic of 1952, Bjørn Ibsen ventilated patients by pushing air into the lungs through a tube, the start of intensive care.

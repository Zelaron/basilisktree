Robert Dennard at IBM devised the one-transistor, one-capacitor memory cell in 1966 and patented it in 1968, storing each bit as charge on a tiny capacitor that must be refreshed. Intel's 1103 chip of 1970 was the first widely sold DRAM and soon replaced magnetic-core memory, and DRAM remains the main working memory of computers.

General Electric introduced the silicon-controlled rectifier in 1957, a solid-state switch that stays off until a small pulse at its gate turns it on, and then conducts large currents. Thyristors began solid-state power electronics, controlling motors, light dimmers, welders and high-voltage DC links without moving parts.

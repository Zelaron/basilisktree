Isamu Akasaki and Hiroshi Amano made the first gallium nitride blue LEDs in Nagoya in 1989, and Shuji Nakamura at Nichia made bright, efficient ones in 1993. Blue light completed the red and green LEDs, and with a phosphor it gave white light at a fraction of the energy of incandescent bulbs. The three shared the 2014 Nobel Prize in Physics.

John Leal, with the engineer George Fuller, began continuous chlorination of Jersey City's water supply in 1908, the first on this scale in the United States. Together with filtration it sharply cut typhoid and other waterborne disease in the cities that adopted it, and it is credited with much of the fall in urban death rates in the early twentieth century.

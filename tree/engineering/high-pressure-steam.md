Richard Trevithick in Cornwall and Oliver Evans in the United States built steam engines that worked at pressures well above atmospheric around 1801. They were far smaller and lighter than Watt's low-pressure engines, light enough to move themselves, and Trevithick's locomotive hauled a train at Penydarren in Wales in 1804.

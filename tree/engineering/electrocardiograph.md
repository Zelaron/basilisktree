Willem Einthoven used a string galvanometer to record the electrical activity of the human heart and published his method in 1903, naming the waves of the trace P, Q, R, S and T. The electrocardiogram became the basic tool for diagnosing arrhythmias and heart attacks, and Einthoven received the 1924 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

Honoré Blanc in France tried in the 1780s to make interchangeable musket parts, though they were still filed by hand. In the 1820s Simeon North's milling machines and John H. Hall's gauges in the United States turned out truly interchangeable rifle parts. Guns, then sewing machines, clocks and cars could then be assembled without fitting, which made mass production possible.

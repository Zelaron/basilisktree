Shen Kuo described a magnetized needle that points south in 1088 and noted that it does not point exactly at true south, and Chinese ships were steering by compass by the early twelfth century. The compass spread to the Islamic world and Europe, giving sailors a direction that did not depend on the sun or the stars.

Károly Zipernowsky, Ottó Bláthy and Miksa Déri at the Ganz Works in Budapest built closed-iron-core transformers in 1885, and William Stanley made a practical one for the Westinghouse system in 1886. A transformer steps alternating voltage up for long-distance transmission and down for use, which made high-voltage AC grids possible.

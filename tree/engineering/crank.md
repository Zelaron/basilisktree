A water-powered stone sawmill at Hierapolis in Asia Minor, built in the second half of the third century CE, is the earliest known machine to use a crank and connecting rod to turn rotation into back-and-forth motion. The same linkage later converted the piston's push into rotation in steam and petrol engines.

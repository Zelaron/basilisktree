René Laennec, a French physician, first rolled paper into a tube to listen to a patient's chest in 1816 and then made a wooden tube, the first stethoscope. Listening to the heart and lungs let doctors link sounds to disease, checked against autopsy findings, and began the routine use of instruments in diagnosis.

Canals built by the Samarra culture around 6000 BCE carried river water to fields in Mesopotamia, which let farmers grow crops where rainfall alone was too low. Irrigation fed the first cities, and irrigated land is about a fifth of the world's cropland and yields roughly two fifths of its food.

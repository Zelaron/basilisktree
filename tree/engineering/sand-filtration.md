James Simpson built slow sand filters at the Chelsea Waterworks in London in 1829, passing Thames water through beds of sand to clear it. In the cholera epidemic of 1892, filtered Altona had far fewer cases than neighbouring Hamburg, which drew unfiltered river water, and filtration became standard for city supplies.

Francis Herbert Wenham designed and ran the first enclosed wind tunnel in 1871 for the Aeronautical Society of Great Britain, blowing air past models to measure lift and drag. Tunnel tests let designers study wings without risking flights, and the Wright brothers' tunnel experiments in 1901 corrected earlier published data and fixed their wing design.

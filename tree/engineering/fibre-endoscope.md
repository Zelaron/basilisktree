Harold Hopkins and Narinder Singh Kapany showed in 1954 that a bundle of glass fibres could carry an image, and in 1957 Basil Hirschowitz used a flexible fibre-optic gastroscope on a patient in Michigan. Doctors could now look inside the stomach and bowel without surgery, which began minimally invasive medicine.

The Aqua Appia, begun in 312 BCE by the censor Appius Claudius Caecus, was the first of the eleven aqueducts that brought water to Rome by gravity along underground channels and arcades. Roman engineers surveyed gentle gradients over long distances, and the aqueducts supplied baths, fountains and private houses for centuries.

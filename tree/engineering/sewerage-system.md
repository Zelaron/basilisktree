Hamburg began planned sewers in the 1840s to a design by William Lindley, and Joseph Bazalgette's intercepting sewers carried London's sewage east of the city from 1865, after the Great Stink of 1858. Sewer networks carried waste away from homes and water supplies, which helped end London's cholera epidemics, and became the model for city sanitation.

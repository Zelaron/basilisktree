William Thomson's machine of 1872 to 1873 added up tidal harmonics with pulleys and a wire to trace the predicted height of the tide at a port, a mechanical use of Fourier analysis. Larger machines followed in 1876 and 1879, and tide predictors stayed in service until digital computers replaced them in the 1960s and 1970s.

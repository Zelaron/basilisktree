Louis Braille, blind since a childhood accident, devised a cell of six raised dots at the age of fifteen (1824) and published the system in 1829. He adapted it from Charles Barbier's twelve-dot night writing. The 64 possible patterns form a fixed-length binary code that is read by touch, and braille is still in use worldwide.

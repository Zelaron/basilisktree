Shafi Goldwasser, Silvio Micali and Charles Rackoff, and independently László Babai (1985), defined proofs in which a randomized verifier questions a powerful prover. Zero-knowledge proofs let the prover convince the verifier without revealing anything else. The work won the 1993 Gödel Prize, and Goldwasser and Micali received the 2012 Turing Award.

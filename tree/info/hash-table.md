Hans Peter Luhn's IBM memorandum of January 1953 stored records in buckets chosen by applying a function to the key, chaining the collisions. At IBM, Gene Amdahl and colleagues used open addressing with linear probing for an assembler in the 1950s. Hash tables give expected constant-time lookup and underlie dictionaries in most programming languages.

John Cocke's IBM 801 (1980), the Berkeley RISC project of David Patterson (1981) and Stanford's MIPS of John Hennessy (1981) showed that a small set of simple, pipelined instructions runs faster than a large complex one. ARM and RISC-V descend from the approach, and Hennessy and Patterson received the 2017 Turing Award.

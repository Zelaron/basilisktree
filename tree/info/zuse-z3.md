Konrad Zuse's Z3, presented in Berlin on 12 May 1941, was the first working programmable, fully automatic digital computer. It used about 2,600 telephone relays, binary floating-point numbers and programs punched on film, but had no conditional branch. The original was destroyed in an air raid in December 1943.

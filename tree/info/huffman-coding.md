David Huffman, then a graduate student at MIT, devised a method that builds an optimal prefix code from symbol frequencies by repeatedly merging the two rarest symbols (a term paper of 1951, published 1952). It beat the Shannon–Fano code taught by his professor and is a component of JPEG, MP3, ZIP and fax compression.

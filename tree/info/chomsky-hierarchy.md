Noam Chomsky's papers of 1956 and 1959 ranked formal grammars into regular, context-free, context-sensitive and unrestricted types, recognized by finite automata, pushdown automata, linear bounded automata and Turing machines. Context-free grammars became the standard way to define programming-language syntax.
